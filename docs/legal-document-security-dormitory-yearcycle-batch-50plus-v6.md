## Atomic rules — 82

### Student protection, expected conduct and awards — Md.157–162

1. Student conduct/protection is a shared school-management workflow involving administration, teachers, guidance service, parent, school-family association and relevant stakeholders.
2. Student conduct expectations must be represented as explicit school-rule controls, not free-text only.
3. Compliance with law, social values and school rules is an explicit student obligation.
4. Protection of school/public property is a student obligation.
5. Alcohol/addictive substance use or possession is prohibited under the student conduct model.
6. Gambling and gambling-like environments are prohibited under the student conduct model.
7. Regular attendance is an explicit expected behaviour.
8. Environmental and historical-environment protection is an explicit expected behaviour.
9. Student responsibilities in school, dormitory, workplace, transport and related environments must remain linked to the same student conduct profile where applicable.
10. Student protection actions must be able to trigger guidance/support rather than only discipline.
11. Positive conduct and achievement are eligible for reward workflows under the regulation.
12. Reward eligibility must be evaluated using the regulation's applicable academic/behaviour criteria rather than a tenant-created arbitrary threshold.
13. Honour/commendation-style outcomes must preserve the legal basis and evaluation evidence.
14. Reward decisions must be stored with school year and decision provenance.
15. Reward records must not overwrite historical years.
16. Award documents are issued to the student/parent through an accountable delivery workflow.
17. Reward delivery may be ceremonial and should support evidence of delivery.
18. Honour/commendation lists are year-bound outputs.
19. Published school honour/commendation lists must preserve the legal publication period rather than remain indefinitely active.
20. Reward and discipline data must remain separate dimensions of the student profile even where both affect conduct evaluation.

### Discipline cross-validation — Md.163–164

21. Discipline sanction types are kınama, short-term suspension, school change and removal from formal education.
22. A community-service activity may replace kınama when the regulation permits it.
23. Community-service substitution for kınama is not written to e-Okul as a discipline penalty.
24. Kınama and short-term suspension require school-principal approval after board decision.
25. School-change sanction requires district student discipline board approval.
26. Removal from formal education requires provincial student discipline board approval.
27. Discipline offence classification must map each conduct to its legally prescribed sanction band.
28. IT-device misuse can constitute a discipline event and should be evidence-linkable.
29. Failure to comply with health/safety measures can constitute a discipline event.
30. Dormitory conduct events must be eligible for discipline linkage where the regulation expressly covers them.

### Damage, temporary measure and dormitory interface — Md.174–175

31. Damage compensation is separate from the disciplinary sanction itself.
32. School/person property damage must create a receivable/compensation workflow against the responsible student's parent/guardian as legally applicable.
33. Difficulty/refusal in damage payment must route to the applicable public-loss recovery process rather than informal collection.
34. The principal may impose a temporary removal measure in urgent discipline cases subject to legal conditions.
35. Initial temporary removal cannot exceed ten business days.
36. National/provincial education authority notification must be recorded when the temporary measure is used.
37. A student under temporary removal is not admitted to lessons, exams or other activities during the measure.
38. Time spent under this temporary measure is not counted as absence.
39. Discipline proceedings must start no later than three business days after the temporary measure.
40. After referral to the school reward/discipline board, the case must be decided within ten business days.
41. If the legal decision deadline is missed, the temporary measure automatically lapses.
42. Where justified/compelling reasons continue, the temporary measure may be extended only through the legally required education-authority approval.
43. Extension of the temporary measure is limited to two additional extensions.
44. If the discipline case is still unresolved, school-attendance and dormitory-use status require separate competent-authority approval.
45. In extraordinary student-caused situations, the principal may propose temporary closure of school/dormitory annexes up to five business days.

### Inspection, HR and institution lifecycle — Md.207–211

46. Education, teaching and administration activities are subject to guidance/inspection by legally authorized inspection units.
47. Inspection evidence should be attachable to workflows and corrective actions.
48. Student dress-code compliance must reference the current dedicated dress regulation, not a hardcoded local rule pretending to be statutory.
49. Teacher selection/appointment in applicable school types must defer to the current teacher appointment/relocation regulation.
50. Opening, closing and naming of secondary education institutions must follow the dedicated opening/closing/naming rules.
51. Institution lifecycle changes must be effective-dated and must not rewrite historical workflow instances.
52. Official seal operations must follow the Official Seal Regulation.
53. Official-seal custody/use should be modelled as an auditable document-security responsibility.

### Cooperation, region coordination and professional development — Md.212–215

54. Schools may cooperate with higher-education institutions, provincial directorates, municipalities, businesses, NGOs, sports clubs and other public/private bodies for authorized educational purposes.
55. Cooperation may cover curriculum development, in-service training, improvement of educational environments, production, seminars and projects.
56. External tools/equipment, teaching personnel and other resources may be used through properly governed cooperation.
57. Cooperation may be formalized by protocol.
58. Protocol records must include parties, scope, validity, approvals and evidence rather than a plain uploaded PDF only.
59. Education-region principals' board must address shared educational needs.
60. Education-region principals' board may coordinate shared use of educational environments/resources.
61. Education-region principals' board must support education-quality improvement and implementation consistency.
62. In-service training activities must route to the applicable current in-service-training legislation.
63. School-family association activities must route to the applicable current School-Family Association Regulation.
64. School-family association workflows must remain legally separate from school-budget/management workflows while allowing relations.

### Scholarship, boarding, social aid and half-boarding — Md.216–217

65. Scholarship, boarding, social aid and school-dormitory services are governed by their dedicated regulation; OÖKY acts as a routing provision here.
66. Dormitory workflow activation must depend on the institution's dormitory/boarding feature flag.
67. Economically disadvantaged students identified by class guidance teachers may receive lunch support from the applicable half-boarding appropriation.
68. Eligibility assessment for lunch support must preserve the identifying teacher/evidence and school year.
69. Managers, teachers, other personnel and requesting students may use the meal service by paying the applicable daily tariff where the regulation permits.
70. Inspectors, Ministry-assigned personnel and visiting teacher/student groups may use the meal service under the applicable tariff conditions.
71. Meal fees are collected in advance at the beginning of each month.
72. Unconsumed-meal fees are normally not refunded or carried over.
73. If the institution is closed for at least one week, the corresponding prepaid meal amount is carried to the following month's account.
74. In institutions with a dormitory, materials acquired from half-boarding appropriations are transferred to the dormitory warehouse.
75. A warehouse ledger must be maintained for transferred half-boarding materials.

### School security, archive, official correspondence and electronic records — Md.218–221

76. School management must establish measures for a safe school environment and protection from physical and psychological violence.
77. Communication tools, camera and alarm systems may be used as part of legally compliant school-security measures.
78. Security personnel may be assigned for school safety.
79. Security measures must be represented as preventive controls with responsible person, evidence and review cycle.
80. Institutional records must be archived according to the applicable archive legislation.
81. Official correspondence must be conducted under the applicable official-correspondence legislation.
82. Processes that can be carried out electronically should be carried out electronically; records that cannot be electronically archived must be printed, approved and retained according to procedure.
